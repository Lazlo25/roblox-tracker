How it works

Everything runs in the cloud. Every hour a GitHub job reads the public Roblox charts and records each game’s CCU, total visits and votes, plus RoTerminal’s count of everyone playing Roblox and its players by genre. At 1 PM Eastern a second job stores the four public charts (All Devices, All Locations), with names, genres and creation dates from Roblox’s public games API. A Claude run every 4 hours (11 PM, 3, 7 and 11 AM, 3 and 7 PM Eastern) brings the day’s figures into this page, and the 3 PM run also updates the Deep Analysis. 18+ games appear only on signed-in charts, so they aren’t tracked. A game’s CCU history covers every day it was recorded.

Columns in every chart

Position
Place on that chart today, with its move since the previous snapshot or a New tag. Top Trending lists only games up to 4 months old; they keep their real chart position, so its numbering has gaps, and games that age out leave without becoming Fallen Heroes.
Avg CCU (day)
Concurrent players averaged over the day’s hourly readings, counted from midnight Eastern. At the 1 PM snapshot it covers midnight to 1 PM; the 4-hourly updates extend it, and the 3 AM run finalizes it with the full midnight-to-midnight day. Hover a value for the hours covered, their low–high and the snapshot reading. A game needs readings in at least half the hours so far (12 for a finished day); otherwise it shows its snapshot reading, in italics, as do all days before the hourly job started. Every column built on CCU (history, the CCU change columns, Monetization Score) uses this value
Created
The date the experience was created on Roblox.
History
Days in a row the game has stayed on that particular chart; it restarts at 1 if the game drops off and returns, and differs from chart to chart.
Session length
Minutes per visit, measured from Roblox’s own counts: the game’s average players over the day divided by its visits per minute (Little’s law), using its visit count at the day’s first and latest hourly readings. It needs at least 6 hours of readings. The small figure is the change since the game’s previous recorded day.
Like %
Share of the game’s votes that are thumbs-up, read every hour from Roblox’s votes API; the day’s latest reading is shown, with its change in percentage points since the previous recorded day.
CCU change
All four columns compare market share: a game’s daily CCU divided by everyone playing Roblox that day (RoTerminal’s day average), so weekday swings, holidays and platform-wide growth or decline cancel out.
vs same day last week
Today’s share compared with the same weekday a week earlier, which also cancels the game’s own weekly rhythm. Blank until the game has a reading from exactly 7 days ago.
Last 7 days vs prior 7
Average share over the last 7 recorded days compared with the 7 before.
vs first 7 days
Average share over the last 7 days compared with the game’s first 7 recorded days.
vs peak 7 days
Average share over the last 7 days compared with its highest 7-day average. “Peak” means the latest week is the high.
Under 14 days
Until a game has 14 recorded days, its history is split into two equal halves and those averages are compared instead; a small superscript (for example 2d) shows the window length used.

Monetization estimate (Top Earning only)

Monetization Score
Rank of chart position × today’s Avg CCU (day), using nothing else: 1 is the game holding the highest earning position for the fewest players.
Est. revenue / month
Chart position sets the estimate: yearly revenue ≈ C × position−1.084, a curve that matches Roblox’s reported creator averages for the 12 months to June 30, 2026 (top 10: $65.7M, top 100: $10.8M, top 1,000: $1.4M), scaled so the top 10 average $68M a year, then divided by 12. CCU nudges it: a game with more players than is typical for its position moves toward the game above, fewer moves it toward the game below, never by more than half a position or 10%, so the estimates keep the chart’s order.

Fallen Heroes

When a game drops off a chart it moves to that chart’s Fallen Heroes list, showing its name, CCU, creation date and the days in a row it had stayed on the chart. Its CCU keeps being tracked for 3 days after it leaves. After that tracking stops and only its name stays, faded, until it returns to the chart.

Deep Analysis

Every day the task also reads each charted game’s public page (description, game passes and developer products with prices, badges, media, likes, favorites, visits, update date, creator) and profiles the gameplay of new or changed games from their descriptions and thumbnails. A script then measures each chart’s pillar, scans every collected trait against it (checking whether one creator, game age or a single threshold drives the result), and re-tests every hypothesis. Confidence rises only when a pattern holds across more games and more days, and each hypothesis is marked New, Strengthened, Weakened, Unchanged or Retired against the day before. The card shows each chart’s title keywords and the hypotheses that point to something a developer can do, worded as short steps with their confidence; hover a step for the hypothesis and evidence behind it. Findings describe associations, not proven causes.

Overview

Title keywords
Words in game titles, ignoring tags in brackets such as [UPD] and common filler words, with the share of each chart’s titles that use them.
Movers
The strip at the top: today’s biggest rises and falls in market share among charted games with at least 1,000 players, against each game’s previous recorded day. Click one to open it in its chart.
Players on Roblox
Everyone playing Roblox at once, from RoTerminal: the latest hourly reading, the day averages and peaks RoTerminal publishes for every day, and the hourly readings of the last 72 hours.
Genre fit
Each chart’s share of games in a genre (Roblox’s own genre for each game) divided by that genre’s share of players, averaged over the day’s hourly readings of RoTerminal’s top 600 games, which use the same Roblox genres. It is read hourly, so it replaces Dubit’s genre tracker (a multi-month share of plays with its own genre names), which stays as the fallback when RoTerminal’s figures are missing. It is not a probability: it shows which genres a chart favors relative to how much they’re played.
Entries and Fallen Heroes
Games that joined or dropped off each chart since its previous snapshot.
Chart overlap
How many of the four charts each of today’s games appears on, with the games on three or all four listed by Top Earning position and tagged with their charts.

Averaging the day’s hourly readings removes most of the time-of-day effect, so games whose players live in other time zones are no longer flattered or penalized by when the snapshot runs; update days and events still move a day’s average. Roblox’s tier averages are per creator, not per game. Revenue figures are for comparing games, not exact earnings.
