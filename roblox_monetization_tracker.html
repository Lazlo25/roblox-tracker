How it works

At 1 PM Eastern a cloud job reads the four public Roblox charts (All Devices, All Locations) and stores every game on them; at 2 PM a Claude task on the owner’s Mac reads the signed-in charts and adds the 18+ titles the public charts leave out, and a cloud Claude run then updates the Deep Analysis. Names, genres and creation dates come from Roblox’s public games API at the same moment, and Dubit’s share of plays by genre is re-read then too. Separately, a cloud job reads the charts every hour and records each game’s CCU, so each day’s CCU is the average of that day’s hourly readings rather than one reading. A game’s CCU history covers every day it was recorded.

Columns in every chart

Position
Place on that chart today, with its move since the previous snapshot or a New tag. Top Trending lists only games up to 4 months old; they keep their real chart position, so its numbering has gaps, and games that age out leave without becoming Fallen Heroes.
Avg CCU (day)
Concurrent players averaged over the day’s hourly readings, counted from midnight Eastern. At the 1 PM snapshot it covers midnight to 1 PM; updates at about 7:30 and 11:30 PM extend it, and the next afternoon’s run finalizes it with the full midnight-to-midnight day. Hover a value for the hours covered, their low–high and the snapshot reading. A game needs readings in at least half the hours so far (12 for a finished day); otherwise it shows its snapshot reading, in italics, as do all days before the hourly job started. Every column built on CCU (history, the CCU change columns, Monetization Score) uses this value
Created
The date the experience was created on Roblox.
History
Days the game has been recorded on that chart.
CCU change
All four columns compare market share: a game’s daily CCU divided by that day’s combined CCU of the 100 biggest games, so weekday swings and platform-wide growth or decline cancel out.
vs same day last week
Today’s share compared with the same weekday a week earlier, which also cancels the game’s own weekly rhythm. Blank until the game has a reading from exactly 7 days ago.
Last 7 days vs prior 7
Average share over the last 7 recorded days compared with the 7 before.
vs first 7 days
Average share over the last 7 days compared with the game’s first 7 recorded days.
vs peak 7 days
Average share over the last 7 days compared with its highest 7-day average. “Peak” means the latest week is the high.
Under 14 days
Until a game has 14 recorded days, its history is split into two equal halves and those averages are compared instead; a small superscript (for example 2d) shows the window length used.

Monetization estimate (Top Earning only)

Monetization Score
Rank of chart position × today’s Avg CCU (day), using nothing else: 1 is the game holding the highest earning position for the fewest players.
Est. revenue / month
Chart position sets the estimate: yearly revenue ≈ C × position−1.084, a curve that matches Roblox’s reported creator averages for the 12 months to June 30, 2026 (top 10: $65.7M, top 100: $10.8M, top 1,000: $1.4M), scaled so the top 10 average $68M a year, then divided by 12. CCU nudges it: a game with more players than is typical for its position moves toward the game above, fewer moves it toward the game below, never by more than half a position or 10%, so the estimates keep the chart’s order.

Fallen Heroes

When a game drops off a chart it moves to that chart’s Fallen Heroes list, showing its name, CCU, creation date and the days it was recorded on the chart. Its CCU keeps being tracked for 3 days after it leaves. After that tracking stops and only its name stays, faded, until it returns to the chart.

Deep Analysis

Every day the task also reads each charted game’s public page (description, game passes and developer products with prices, badges, media, likes, favorites, visits, update date, creator) and profiles the gameplay of new or changed games from their descriptions and thumbnails. A script then measures each chart’s pillar, scans every collected trait against it (checking whether one creator, game age or a single threshold drives the result), and re-tests every hypothesis. Confidence rises only when a pattern holds across more games and more days, and each hypothesis is marked New, Strengthened, Weakened, Unchanged or Retired against the day before. The card shows each chart’s title keywords and the hypotheses that point to something a developer can do, worded as short steps with their confidence; hover a step for the hypothesis and evidence behind it. Findings describe associations, not proven causes.

Overview

Title keywords
Words in game titles, ignoring tags in brackets such as [UPD] and common filler words, with the share of each chart’s titles that use them.
Genre fit
Each chart’s share of games in a genre (Roblox’s own genre for each game) divided by that genre’s share of all Roblox plays, from Dubit’s genre tracker, which the daily task re-reads at every snapshot. It is not a probability: neither source gives the number of games in each genre. It shows which genres a chart favors relative to how much they’re played.
Entries and Fallen Heroes
Games that joined or dropped off each chart since its previous snapshot.
Chart overlap
How many of the four charts each of today’s games appears on, with the games on three or all four listed by Top Earning position and tagged with their charts.

Averaging the day’s hourly readings removes most of the time-of-day effect, so games whose players live in other time zones are no longer flattered or penalized by when the snapshot runs; update days and events still move a day’s average. Roblox’s tier averages are per creator, not per game. Revenue figures are for comparing games, not exact earnings.

Sources: Roblox charts and games API; creator earnings via TechSpot and Dexerto.
